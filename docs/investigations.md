# Investigations

Shorter investigations into how the game behaves, including the ones that did not find the answer. Each was done with a temporary diagnostic that is not published here; the probes that are published with their code are in [research](../research/).

## Gas Probe: a crash after a gas grenade

**Question.** The game crashed in a repeatable way after a gas grenade went off among NPCs. What happens in the moment before that crash? **Method.** A temporary redscript probe wrapped the grenade's life (throw, detonation, the attacks it spawns, release, despawn) and the NPC events around it (status effects applied and removed, damage taken, defeat, death, ragdoll start, impacts and end). Every line was written to disk at once, and each call into the game and other mods was bracketed by a line before and a line after, so a "before" line without its "after" line would name the call the game died in. While a grenade was live, a tick line every 0.25 seconds would date a crash to a quarter of a second. **Result.** The crash did not occur while the logging was on, which points to a timing-dependent fault, and its cause was not found. The log did answer a different question on the side: why an electric area quickhack set up to knock people out still killed them. A thrown target hit the ground at 17.8 m/s without the non-lethal mark, so the game's ragdoll-impact rule marked it for death, and a bystander knocked out by the burst was moved to the defeated state and then killed by the next small damage tick. That finding led to the own Thunderclap quickhack, written from scratch to deal no game damage to humans and to apply the knocked-out state directly.

## Zoom Diag: optics zoom and a hidden HUD

**Question.** Two faults: the optics zoom (right mouse button plus scroll wheel) changed the zoom level, visible because NPC health bars moved, but the camera did not zoom; and the HUD stayed hidden after scanning or quickhack targeting until something else was scanned, with a HUD-hiding mod installed. **Method.** A read-only Cyber Engine Tweaks script sampled the zoom, scanner, device, camera and HUD state four times a second, logged every change, and wrote a full state dump automatically when the zoom level was raised but the camera did not follow or when the HUD mod's flags disagreed. A later version also read the visibility and opacity of each HUD widget of that mod. **Result.** The zoom question was answered: the zoom fails only while a melee weapon or an arm cyberware weapon is drawn. The HUD question stayed open: the HUD mod's own global toggle did not bring the HUD back either, and no cause was recorded from the per-widget readout.

## Vehicle Restriction Probe: arms that could not be drawn after a metro ride

**Question.** After riding the metro, the player could draw guns but no arm cyberware or other abilities. Which restriction stayed stuck, and why? **Method.** A small Cyber Engine Tweaks logger (144 lines) recorded every entry into and exit from the passenger and vehicle-combat states, including forced exits, every call by which an auto-drive mod applied or removed its passenger restriction, every time that restriction was applied or removed, and a check a few seconds after each save load. **Result.** The auto-drive mod applies its passenger restriction when those states begin and removes it only in their normal exit step. The game can also end the states through a forced exit, which skips that step, so the restriction stays on, and it is saved with the game. This led to a small compatibility mod that removes the restriction on a forced exit, with a safety check shortly after leaving a vehicle and after each load. That fix compiles but has not been run in game yet.

## Stutter study: Lua garbage collection

**Question.** A short hitch of 40 to 170 ms returned every few seconds during normal play. Where does it come from? **Method.** An event trace of the whole system (kernel sampling plus frame timing) placed 77 percent of the samples taken during hitches in the Lua runtime of Cyber Engine Tweaks, where about 280 installed script mods share a single Lua state. A diagnostic that stopped the Lua garbage collector for 90 seconds gave flat 8 ms frames, and 277 heap markers, one per mod, plus rounds of disabling half of the mods, showed which mods created the garbage. The [Monitor](../research/monitor/README.md) log dated when the steady stutter began. **Result.** Confirmed: the mods together produced about 10 MB of Lua garbage per second, and each collection cycle stalled the game for 40 to 170 ms. Tuning or stepping the collector was tested and rejected, and a scheduler that runs collections during menus and loading screens did not remove the steady hitch. The fix was to cut per-frame work in about 63 other authors' mods (checks moved from every frame to 5 to 10 times a second, early exits, cached values) with re-apply scripts and backups. The result after the change was judged smoother in play; no before and after numbers were recorded. Those edits are other authors' code and are not published; they are listed in [COMPATIBILITY.md](../COMPATIBILITY.md).
